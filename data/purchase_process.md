# Purchase Process (Procure-to-Pay)

This document is a fictional sample policy for Nile Trading Co., written for learning purposes.

## 1. Purchase requisition
Any employee who needs goods or services creates a purchase requisition in SAP. The requisition must include the item, quantity, estimated cost and the cost center. The employee's direct manager approves or rejects the requisition within 2 working days.

## 2. Approval limits
Purchase orders are approved based on their total value:
- Up to EGP 20,000: approved by the department manager.
- From EGP 20,001 to EGP 250,000: approved by the department manager and the finance manager.
- Above EGP 250,000: approved by the CEO after finance review.

## 3. Supplier selection
For purchases above EGP 50,000, the procurement team must collect at least three written quotations from approved suppliers. The cheapest offer is not always selected; delivery time, quality and payment terms are also compared. New suppliers must be registered in SAP by the procurement team before an order can be placed.

## 4. Purchase order
After approval, the procurement team converts the requisition into a purchase order (PO) in SAP and sends it to the supplier. No goods may be ordered without a PO number.

## 5. Goods receipt
When goods arrive, the warehouse checks the quantity and condition and posts a goods receipt in SAP within 24 hours. Damaged goods are rejected and reported to procurement on the same day.

## 6. Supplier invoice and three-way match
Accounts payable enters the supplier invoice in SAP. The system performs a three-way match between the purchase order, the goods receipt and the invoice. If the price or quantity differs by more than 2%, the invoice is blocked for payment until procurement resolves the difference.

## 7. Payment
Matched invoices are paid on the supplier's payment terms, usually 30 or 60 days. Payments run every Tuesday and Thursday by bank transfer.
